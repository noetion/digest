Today’s stories focus on where AI runs and who controls it: an open-source desktop IDE that brings agents into code review, Google testing TPUs in low Earth orbit, tiny on-device LLMs running on Qualcomm AR hardware, Google’s Gemini placing AI calls from Pixel phones, and a startup hiring Jürgen Schmidhuber to lead a world-model research lab. Each item touches operational tradeoffs, local tooling, novel deployment environments, device constraints, and research hires that steer product direction.

## Whiteboard open-sources an MIT-licensed desktop IDE that embeds agents into code review

- **What happened:** Whiteboard published an MIT-licensed desktop app and GitHub repo that integrates coding agents (e.g., Claude Code, Codex) into a canvas-style review workflow, runs against local checkouts, and includes an AST-aware Rust semantic diff and a WASM plugin system.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers get an open-source SDK and UI to let agents draw diagrams, link traces to code, and jump from visual artifacts to the underlying files while keeping data local; the repo also vendors Code OSS for editor features and exposes telemetry controls.
- **Outlook:** The project’s GitHub will show progress toward the planned hosted team product and usability limits via issue activity, CONTRIBUTING.md updates, and incoming pull requests.

## Google’s Project Suncatcher will test Tensor Processing Units in low Earth orbit on a Transporter: 18 rideshare

- **What happened:** Google announced a prototype satellite carrying Trillium TPUs will launch on the Transporter: 18 rideshare next week to gather in-orbit data on launch vibration, radiation effects, cooling, and satellite interconnects; prior lab tests showed TPUs surviving vibration and proton-beam radiation doses higher than a five-year mission simulation.
- **Why it matters:** This is an engineering feasibility test for placing ML compute in space: it measures whether TPUs survive launch shock and radiation, validates radiator and heat-pipe cooling approaches in vacuum, and will inform multi-satellite laser interconnect designs required for high-bandwidth short-range links.
- **Outlook:** A follow-on 2027 test will place two satellites in orbit to exercise the precision laser links between satellites and evaluate multi-satellite bandwidth and coordination.

## PrismML ports a 2B, 1-bit Bonsai LLM to Qualcomm’s Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 for smart glasses

- **What happened:** PrismML demonstrated a 2-billion-parameter, vision-and-language tuned 1-bit Bonsai LLM running locally on Qualcomm Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 smart-glass hardware at Qualcomm’s Snapdragon Summit; PrismML says the model is a 4x size reduction from larger models while retaining benchmark performance.
- **Why it matters:** This shows an end-to-end on-device LLM optimized for AR hardware and vision-language tasks, enabling real-time local inference on wearable Snapdragon platforms without depending on cloud model serving; no consumer smart-glass product using PrismML has been announced.
- **Outlook:** A commercial smart-glasses product announcement that ships devices running PrismML on Snapdragon hardware would be the next public milestone, since no compatible consumer devices have been announced yet.

<small>Sources: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/prismml-brings-its-tiny-llms-to-qualcomm-powered-smart-glasses/)</small>

## Google rolls out Gemini Call for Me beta on Pixel 11, letting Gemini place business calls in the Phone app

- **What happened:** Google launched Call for Me, an experimental beta exclusive to Pixel 11 owners in the U.S. and English, requiring a Gemini subscription and enrollment in the Phone by Google app public beta; Gemini can call businesses, navigate automated menus, present a live transcript on-device, and let users take over the call at any time.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers and product teams building voice and contact workflows need to account for automated outbound AI interactions tied to device enrollment and subscription gating, and for on-device transcript and takeover semantics that integrate with existing Phone app features like Hold for Me and Direct My Call.
- **Outlook:** Enrollment in the Phone by Google app public beta and Google’s published participation instructions are the immediate signals for which Pixel owners can test Call for Me and report on real-world behavior.

## Sakana AI hires Jürgen Schmidhuber as Chief Scientific Advisor to lead an RSI lab focused on world models

- **What happened:** Sakana AI announced Jürgen Schmidhuber will serve as Chief Scientific Advisor and steer a new RSI lab in Tokyo aimed at building self-reinforcing research loops and world-model–driven projects such as the Darwin Gödel Machine and The AI Scientist.
- **Why it matters:** The hire signals Sakana’s research direction toward world models and recursive self-improvement architectures and represents a deliberate staffing and advisory investment in fundamental-model research centered in Tokyo.
- **Outlook:** Public announcements from Sakana about RSI lab staffing, research outputs, or project milestones like Darwin Gödel Machine or The AI Scientist will show how the advisory hire translates into concrete research programs.
