Three Big Tech developments landed this week: Nvidia’s CEO Jensen Huang resigned amid reports of multiple affairs and a possible national security probe; Anthropic went public and its CEO recommitted to pacing model training and deployment; and Meta said it is rethinking its 2030 net-zero target because AI data center demand drove record emissions. Each item ties to governance, capital allocation, and operational limits for AI infrastructure.

## Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO after reports of multiple affairs and a national security probe

- **What happened:** Nvidia’s board accepted Jensen Huang’s resignation and appointed CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO; reports link one of Huang’s alleged partners to Megaspeed, a firm U.S. officials suspect of channeling Nvidia chips to China, and the Justice Department has taken over an investigation into Megaspeed. Nvidia said it will not reinstate Huang and interim CEO Kress said the company does not plan to sell H200 chips in China.
- **Why it matters:** Nvidia supplies most advanced AI accelerators to hyperscalers and enterprises; leadership disruption coincides with active U.S. probes into chip exports and customer relationships, which could affect export policy compliance, board oversight, and strategic relationships with large buyers such as Megaspeed that reportedly purchased billions of dollars in Nvidia chips.
- **Outlook:** Jensen Huang is expected to testify before Congress in a closed-door briefing recorded and transcribed before Thanksgiving, which falls on November 26, 2026.

## Anthropic debuts on Nasdaq as CEO Dario Amodei reaffirms commitment to pacing AI training and deployment

- **What happened:** Anthropic completed its IPO at the Nasdaq opening bell and CEO Dario Amodei used the event to restate the company’s plan to pace model training and deployment and to ‘remain under human control,’ saying the company will take as long as necessary to have confidence systems are safe.
- **Why it matters:** A public Anthropic converts internal safety commitments into statements to shareholders and regulators; that shifts scrutiny of training cadence and capital allocation into public filings and raises the chance that deployment pacing will appear in forward guidance, SEC disclosures, and public shareholder communications.
- **Outlook:** Anthropic’s post-IPO SEC filings and its next quarterly report will be the first public documents to show whether the company’s pacing commitment affects its training schedules, capital plans, or deployment disclosures.

## Meta says it is re-evaluating its 2030 net-zero goal after record emissions from AI data centers

- **What happened:** In its 2026 sustainability report, Meta reported total emissions rising from 15.6 million metric tonnes CO2e in 2024 to more than 21.7 million metric tonnes in 2025 and said it is re-evaluating timelines, sequencing, and interim milestones including the 2030 net-zero target because demand for AI infrastructure grew faster than expected.
- **Why it matters:** Meta’s public retreat on a hard 2030 net-zero timeline signals that hyperscale AI deployments are materially changing energy and capital planning; engineers and infrastructure teams at cloud and hardware vendors should expect continued reliance on mixed power sources and revised interim intensity targets from major hyperscalers.
- **Outlook:** Meta’s next sustainability update, the 2027 report, should spell out any revised interim intensity targets and a new timetable for its net-zero ambition.
