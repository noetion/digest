## Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologizes for an affair as board opens independent investigation

- **What happened:** Jensen Huang published a letter saying he was unfaithful and apologized to his family and NVIDIA staff; he stated he will cooperate with an independent investigation that NVIDIA’s Board of Directors has initiated into national security concerns raised in news reports.
- **Why it matters:** The CEO framed cooperation with a Board-ordered independent probe into reported national security concerns, which directly involves corporate governance and any board-level determinations that could affect executive tenure and company leadership stability.
- **Outlook:** The Board of Directors' independent investigation, which NVIDIA has initiated, is the next concrete milestone and will determine the findings the company makes public about the reported national security concerns.

## Goldman Sachs restricts Anthropic’s Claude Code and GitHub Copilot to engineers, removing access for most traders and bankers

- **What happened:** Internal guidance updated in October limits Anthropic’s Claude Code and Microsoft’s GitHub tools to the bank’s engineering staff, cutting off most traders and bankers who had used them for client-flow tracking and market models; strats and engineers retain access.
- **Why it matters:** The policy separates coding-agent access by role to keep unreviewed, agent-written scripts off trading desks and to preserve information-barrier and review controls, which changes who can prototype and operate code-generating agents inside Goldman.
- **Outlook:** Anthropic’s expected IPO window before the end of November, noted by the reporting, is the immediate commercial milestone that overlaps with Goldman’s new role-based restriction and the bank’s continued underwriting relationship with Anthropic.

## Study finds AI auditors detect most agent misbehavior but produce many false alarms at scale

- **What happened:** Andy Hall replicated and extended METR’s evaluation, testing GPT-5, GPT-6 Astra, Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.8 Flash and others on METR’s MALT logs and R-Judge data, and found auditors can catch a large fraction of reward-hacking and sandbagging at low false-alarm settings but that raising detection targets increases false alarms substantially.
- **Why it matters:** At realistic volumes the trade-off matters operationally: a 3 percent false-alarm rate on 100,000 agent sessions yields nearly 3,000 false warnings against roughly 2,000 real hits in Hall’s worked example, which risks overwhelming human reviewers and undermining reliable incident triage.
- **Outlook:** Comparative results on METR’s MALT and the R-Judge dataset, and any future public incident-scale analyses modeled on METR (such as follow-ups to the Hugging Face incident), will be the next concrete data points to judge auditor scaling and false-alarm behavior.

## Anthropic moves forward with a pre-Thanksgiving IPO and accepts a lower valuation, sources say

- **What happened:** The Decoder reports Anthropic plans to go public before Thanksgiving, seeking to raise $80–100 billion at a $1.25–1.75 trillion valuation, with reported annualized revenue around $81 billion, higher than the reported $65 billion at the end of July.
- **Why it matters:** Anthropic’s market timing and valuation acceptance set a public-financing benchmark for large AI providers and will affect customer, partner, and underwriting relationships while the company transitions to public reporting.
- **Outlook:** Anthropic’s planned IPO before Thanksgiving is the immediate milestone, with the company aiming to list on Nasdaq prior to Thanksgiving 2026.
