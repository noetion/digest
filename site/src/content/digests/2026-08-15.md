Microsoft is consolidating its consumer and Microsoft 365 Copilot apps and pruning features, while Meta published a 6,500-word AI manifesto alongside an open-weight model release; both moves signal platform repositioning around who gets access to AI and which features companies will keep.

## Microsoft merges consumer and Microsoft 365 Copilot apps and will remove several Copilot features by August 18, 2026

- **What happened:** Microsoft will combine its consumer Copilot app with Microsoft 365 Copilot and remove features including Group Chats, AI-generated podcasts, Copilot Labs experimental features, and Deep Research for consumers by August 18, 2026; files from the stand-alone Copilot app will be migrated to OneDrive. Microsoft will also retire the Copilot animated character Mico and warns some features may temporarily disappear during the transition.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers and product teams who integrate or build on Copilot face changes to available endpoints and user flows: consumer features listed will be removed and content will move to OneDrive, which affects storage, migration, and UX assumptions for applications relying on those Copilot-specific artifacts. Professional users will see Researcher offered as a replacement for Deep Research, which may shift where advanced search and research capabilities surface for paid workflows.
- **Outlook:** August 18, 2026 is the customer-facing cutoff when the listed consumer features stop appearing and the migration to OneDrive is expected to be in effect.

## Mark Zuckerberg publishes a 6,500-word AI manifesto as Meta promotes open-weight Muse Glimmer

- **What happened:** Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg published an approximately 6,500-word essay arguing for broad access to powerful models and positioning open access as an antidote to concentrated AI power; Wired notes Meta announced a new open-weight system called Muse Glimmer where users can download and modify the model.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers tracking model availability and governance should treat Meta’s manifesto and Muse Glimmer release as a public statement of intent for more permissive, downloadable models rather than tighter, closed-hosted approaches, which affects dependency risk, distribution models, and potential replication of work on downstream infrastructure.
- **Outlook:** The public release of Muse Glimmer and any follow-on open-weight model rollouts that Meta announced in conjunction with the manifesto are the next concrete milestones to watch for evidence of Meta’s stated shift toward broad access.
