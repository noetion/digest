Today’s thread: open-model and quantization advances that push large MoE models into phone-sized builds; Google converts its desktop search homepage into an AI-first surface; Bytedance publishes a compact-training paper and open-model results; Anthropic and Stanford publish competing Riemann hypothesis drafts; and Nvidia’s earnings, China export stance, and governance drama remain in tension.

## Inkling: 2-Mini and LookAtMyQuant compress a 26.4B MoE to a 5.4 GB Apple Silicon build

- **What happened:** ShinMegamiBoson published LookAtMyQuant code and model builds that apply trellis-code post-training quantization and a 4,000-step distillation recipe to produce multiple Inkling: 2-Mini builds, including a 5.38 GB build and a 4.72 GB build targeted to fit 12 GB iPhone jetsam ceilings. The repo, under an MIT license, also ships lamq/runtime_mlx tooling and measured benchmarks and decode speeds on Apple M5 Max.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers get an explicit, reproducible post-training quantization pipeline (trellis codes, Fisher-based bit allocation, and layerwise re-quantization plus distillation) with published model files and concrete tradeoffs: the 12.76 GB build matches the BF16 teacher on pooled benchmarks, while the 5.38 GB build preserves 90% 3-task mean and 87% GSM8K at about $34 GPU cost reported for the recipe runs.
- **Outlook:** GitHub releases and the shinmegamiboson/lookatmyquant repo will be the next place to confirm additional packed Inkling: 2-Mini builds and any follow-on tooling updates announced in that repository.

## Google replaces the desktop search button with AI chat shortcuts on the new search homepage

- **What happened:** Google confirmed deployment of a redesigned desktop search homepage that removes the traditional Google Search button and adds AI-connected buttons under the search bar for image generation, file analysis, and Gemini brainstorming, after a multi-week test, according to Ars Technica and a confirmation from Search VP Robby Stein.
- **Why it matters:** The UI change makes Google.com a direct entry point for AI chat and generation workflows rather than a classic query box with a dedicated search button, shifting how engineers and product teams should expect users to enter multi-turn AI interactions from the homepage.
- **Outlook:** Robby Stein's X account and Google's official announcements are the immediate channels Google cited for deployment updates and any subsequent changes to the homepage rollout.

## Bytedance publishes a paper arguing many training phases are unnecessary and backs it with an open-model result

- **What happened:** A Bytedance-led paper and associated team argue that complex, multi-phase training pipelines include steps that can be removed, presenting a compact training approach and an open-model result intended to show competitive performance with fewer phases, per The Decoder coverage.
- **Why it matters:** If reproducible, the paper’s claim and accompanying open-model artifacts could change how engineering teams allocate compute and pipeline stages by foregrounding a shorter pipeline that the authors say retains agentic and multi-step task capability.
- **Outlook:** The public release and distribution of the paper and its experimental artifacts will be the next concrete milestone to validate the claims and measurements reported in The Decoder coverage.

## Anthropic posts a Claude-assisted Lean proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis; Stanford posts an independent human draft

- **What happened:** Anthropic-affiliated mathematician Levent Alpöge published a Claude-assisted proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis encoded in the Lean theorem prover, and hours later Stanford posted an independent, all-human draft proof by Alessandro Masullo, according to The Decoder and related coverage.
- **Why it matters:** Alpöge’s release includes a Lean-encoded, machine-verifiable package that claims computer verification, while Stanford’s draft represents a human-checked traditional approach; both releases force the math and verification communities to perform formal checking and translation work, which engineers working on formal methods and proof assistants will observe closely.
- **Outlook:** Lean verification, community vetting, and the translation of Stanford’s draft into formal systems are the next concrete steps the sources identify as necessary to assess correctness and acceptance.
