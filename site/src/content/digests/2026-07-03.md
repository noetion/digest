Two institutional shifts this week changed how AI gets deployed and paid for: governments enforced and then lifted export controls on Anthropic’s latest models, and Meta is reportedly planning to sell spare AI compute and hosted models as a new cloud business.

## US lifts export curbs on Anthropic’s Fable 5 and Mythos 5 after safety review

- **What happened:** The Commerce Department removed export and in-country transfer licensing requirements for Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 after Anthropic worked with the US government on safety steps. Anthropic confirmed global availability for Fable 5 and said Mythos 5 access for US organizations resumed on June 26, with plans to expand access via its Glasswing program for trusted cybersecurity partners.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers and security teams must expect model release timelines to be influenced by government safety review and partnership commitments, including red-team programs and dedicated 24/7 jailbreak monitoring that Anthropic set up as part of its agreement.
- **Outlook:** The Commerce Department said it reserves the right to re-evaluate and reimpose restrictions, and Anthropic is working with the government to broaden Mythos access under the Glasswing program.

## Meta is reportedly building a cloud business to sell spare AI compute and hosted models

- **What happened:** Bloomberg reported Meta is planning a Meta Compute initiative to sell access to excess AI compute and to host and sell models, including closed-weight models like Muse Spark. The effort would compete with major cloud providers and follows SpaceX and xAI moves to lease spare capacity.
- **Why it matters:** Engineering teams should expect new supply channels for raw GPU and model hosting capacity from nontraditional cloud providers, which could change procurement options and pricing dynamics for large-scale model training and inference.
- **Outlook:** Meta’s initiative is led by infrastructure and AI leaders and remains a reported plan; Bloomberg and TechCrunch say Meta is considering both raw compute sales and hosted models as part of the offering.
