Today’s stories center on AI applied to security and infrastructure: OpenAI released a security-focused model that finds and exploits real vulnerabilities; Palo Alto Networks is negotiating to buy an AI-agent access security startup; Anthropics’ Claude Code raised a privacy bug on GitHub; Baidu open-sourced a 280B-parameter base model designed for long contexts; and a two-person lab published a $21K-trained video diffusion model and a plan for post-training.

## OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber that finds, exploits, and suggests patches for real-world vulnerabilities

- **What happened:** OpenAI announced GPT-6 Cyber, a model trained to find vulnerabilities, produce working exploits, and propose fixes; in tests it found 81 previously unknown severe Firefox bugs and reproduced 1,724 Linux kernel vulnerabilities, producing 948 working exploits and reporting 304 critical findings to maintainers. OpenAI reports benchmark scores including 48.5% on ExploitGym and a 40.3% hit rate for web-app vuln discovery without source access, all from its system card and internal tests.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers should note a vendor-grade model now produces operational exploit proofs and patch suggestions at scale, with OpenAI reporting automated scout-and-analyst agents that scanned 5,034 kernel locations and cost $1.21M total compute for the Linux run. The model’s outputs and integration into reporting channels mean maintainers and defenders will need new workflows for triage, forensics, and verifying machine-generated fixes.
- **Outlook:** OpenAI will expand access gradually from selected Daybreak Red customers to broader Daybreak tiers as it scales the program and says it plans to make the Daybreak for Frontline Defenders funding and a Daybreak Red subgroup available for critical-infrastructure testing.

## Palo Alto Networks in talks to acquire AI-agent security startup Clutch for about $500M

- **What happened:** TechCrunch reports Palo Alto Networks is negotiating to buy Clutch Security for $500 million in cash plus milestone payouts; Clutch, founded three years ago, was last valued at $85 million and is approaching $10 million ARR, implying roughly a 50x ARR multiple if the deal completes.
- **Why it matters:** For engineers and operators, the reported deal signals incumbent network-security vendors are acquiring technology that controls AI agents’ access to corporate systems, which could influence how agent access controls, identity checks, and policy enforcement integrate into existing firewall and CASB stacks.
- **Outlook:** Completion of the acquisition or a formal Palo Alto announcement or regulatory filing will confirm the transaction and its integration plans, which TechCrunch frames as part of Palo Alto’s series of acquisitions this year.

## Anthropic’s Claude Code reported to leak user email addresses in User-Agent headers; built-in privacy mod added in v2.1.317

- **What happened:** A GitHub issue reports Claude Code sent the user’s personal email in the User-Agent header while performing web requests, exposing the address to third parties; the repository thread records a built-in privacy mod named cc-plugin-privacy was introduced in v2.1.317 and later to deterministically block this behavior and is enabled by default.
- **Why it matters:** Developers embedding web-capable agents or running Claude Code locally should treat agent-origin headers as a potential PII leak vector and account for repository-level changes to the client; the vendor has added a default privacy plugin but the issue thread shows community concern and small-scale mitigations such as local hooks.
- **Outlook:** Publication of the cc-plugin-privacy source or further commits to the Anthropic/Claude Code repository will show whether the built-in mod’s checks are fully auditable and complete the mitigation promised in the issue thread.

<small>Sources: [github.com](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/107757)</small>

## Baidu open-sources ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base, a 280B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with long-context training

- **What happened:** Baidu released ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base with 280 billion parameters and a MoE design that activates 14 billion parameters per input; the model was pre-trained on 33.5 trillion tokens with training context up to 128,000 tokens and weights are available on Hugging Face in FP8 and BF16. Baidu says a post-trained version will follow.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers building long-context systems or fine-tuning large foundation models get an open base with extreme context targets and MoE sparsity details; the availability of FP8 and BF16 weights enables cost-optimized deployments and research into scaling long-context inference and fine-tuning to reach the stated up-to-one-million-token capability.
- **Outlook:** Baidu’s promised post-trained ERNIE 5.5 release will be the next milestone to evaluate how the base weights are adapted for downstream tasks and whether the one-million-token inference goal is supported in released checkpoints.

<small>Sources: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/baidu-releases-ernie-5-5-flash-base-with-280-billion-parameters-as-an-open-source-foundation-model/)</small>

## Two-person lab publishes Pyramid-JiT video diffusion model trained for about $21K and details a post-training plan

- **What happened:** Linum published Field Notes on Pyramid-JiT (P-JiT), a pixel-space diffusion architecture trained over ~660M samples on 64 H100s, reporting convergence improvements and a pressure test run costing roughly $21,000; the authors describe architecture, training schedules, hardware (64× H100), and explicit limitations such as poor rendered-text handling and multi-entity scenes.
- **Why it matters:** The post provides concrete training recipes, hardware counts, and optimizer and EMA parameters that engineers can reproduce or adapt for efficient video-model experiments; it also highlights the unresolved area of diffusion post-training and specific hyperparameters used in large-resolution transitions.
- **Outlook:** Linum says it will publish more on its post-training stack and methods in the coming weeks, which will detail their Finite Difference Flow Optimization pipeline and human-in-the-loop post-training steps.
