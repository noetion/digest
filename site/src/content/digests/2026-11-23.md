Today’s briefs connect advances in model capability, money flows into closed-source AI, evaluation norms, real-world agent limits, and product-level privacy defenses: GPT-6.2 Astra produced a machine-verifiable proof; large investors realized paper gains when Anthropic IPOed; a workplace simulation shows coding agents finish under 10% of tasks; OpenAI argued for perplexity as a shared metric; and Signal added screenshot restrictions on mobile.

## GPT-6.2 Astra produced a machine-verifiable proof of Smoktunowicz's gap theorem

- **What happened:** OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra generated a formalized, Lean-checked proof of Smoktunowicz's gap theorem on Autoform-Hard, producing an 1,800-line proof after 94 minutes and $21.58 of runtime; Vals AI says this is the first known Lean proof and Astra now leads the AutoformBench leaderboard with 49 solved tasks.
- **Why it matters:** This is a concrete capability milestone: a frontier model produced a machine-verifiable proof and invented a different proof strategy when Mathlib lacked foundations, showing models can both discover approaches and emit formal artifacts that integrate with proof assistants.
- **Outlook:** AutoformBench leaderboard updates will show whether Astra's 49-task lead over GPT-6.2 Sol and Claude Fable 5.2 (48 each) holds in subsequent benchmark runs.

## Notable Capital, GIC, and Baillie Gifford generated large paper gains after Anthropic's IPO

- **What happened:** TechCrunch reports Notable Capital, GIC, and Baillie Gifford were major pre-IPO backers of Anthropic; Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in its IPO and reached a fully diluted valuation near $1.5 trillion, producing roughly 15x paper returns for some early investors.
- **Why it matters:** Large pre-IPO allocations by institutional investors produced outsized paper returns, concentrating public capital and making Anthropic a substantial public player whose valuation and funding commitments will influence enterprise procurement and competitive funding dynamics.
- **Outlook:** Anthropic's first post-IPO public filings and near-term trading performance will provide the first concrete data points on revenue recognition and market reception now that shares are trading.

## AI coding agents score high on benchmarks but finish fewer than one in ten tasks in a simulated workplace

- **What happened:** The Decoder ran coding agents inside a simulated software company with project boards, chat histories, and colleagues; even top models such as Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra fully completed fewer than 10% of tasks in that environment.
- **Why it matters:** Benchmarks alone do not reflect deployment complexity: agents that saturate isolated tests fail most end-to-end task flows when they must coordinate with project context, interactions, and long-running task states, implying engineering effort is required to convert benchmark accuracy into reliable digital-employee behavior.
- **Outlook:** Follow-up publications or updated simulated-workplace runs from The Decoder or equivalent labs will be the next public checks on whether agent completion rates improve when frameworks or model versions change.

<small>Sources: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/ai-coding-agents-ace-isolated-tests-but-complete-fewer-than-one-in-ten-tasks-in-a-simulated-workplace/)</small>

## OpenAI argues for perplexity as a shared metric to pace model development

- **What happened:** OpenAI published a post advocating using perplexity (cross-entropy loss) on held-out data as a standardized metric for pretraining progress, reporting internal perplexity trends and suggesting industry transparency and shared limits tied to that metric.
- **Why it matters:** Perplexity is proposed as a single, trackable substrate metric linking pretraining progress to latent capabilities; if adopted, it would change how developers and policymakers compare base models and set engineering thresholds tied to risk and monitoring practices.
- **Outlook:** OpenAI's prediction that transformative pretraining advances could appear by 2027 sets that year as the concrete window for any reported industry movement on perplexity transparency or commitments to joint pacing measures.

<small>Sources: [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/)</small>

## Signal limits mobile screenshots, citing OS-level AI agents and surveillance risks

- **What happened:** Signal announced it will restrict screenshots on Android and iOS as it already does on Windows, saying OS-level agentic AI that can 'scan' screens undermines Signal's ability to guarantee privacy; users can disable the restriction, which Signal says would 'break our privacy guarantees.'
- **Why it matters:** A major privacy-focused app is adjusting product behavior specifically because of agentic OS-level AI, forcing engineering trade-offs between user control and built-in defenses against system-level data capture.
- **Outlook:** Android and iOS client updates that implement Signal's screenshot restrictions are the next concrete product milestones to watch as the company expands the policy beyond Windows.
