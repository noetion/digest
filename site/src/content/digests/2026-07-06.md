Today’s stories show institutions adjusting to AI at scale: a legacy crowdsourcing platform stopping new signups, a European accelerator pushing commercialization, firms locking down third-party models, and a regulator asking for new powers.

## AWS stops accepting new customers for Mechanical Turk, existing users remain supported

- **What happened:** Amazon announced Mechanical Turk will close to new customers on July 30, 2026; existing customers can continue using the service and Amazon does not plan to add new features.
- **Why it matters:** Researchers and engineers who relied on Mechanical Turk for human annotation must not onboard new projects there and should plan alternative annotation pipelines or vendors because Amazon will not expand the service.
- **Outlook:** Watch for announcements from Amazon about an eventual full shutdown or migration options, and for third-party annotation services pitching replacements.

## Station F expands F/ai accelerator with new partners to push startups toward €1M revenue targets

- **What happened:** Station F plans a second F/ai cohort in September, adding partners including Eleven Labs, Nebius, Rippling, OpenRouter, HubSpot, and GitHub; the program targets €1 million revenue per startup within six months.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers at European AI startups gain faster access to partner integrations, potential equity investors, and commercialization support, which can shorten time-to-revenue compared with more research-focused accelerators.
- **Outlook:** Track cohort outcomes and follow funding and revenue metrics from the second batch to assess whether Station F’s commercialization target holds.

<small>Sources: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/06/station-f-ramps-up-as-a-launchpad-for-europes-hottest-ai-startups/)</small>

## Alibaba reportedly bans employees from using Claude Code as firms tighten internal AI tool policies

- **What happened:** Reporting indicates Alibaba has prohibited employees from using Anthropic’s Claude Code; Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch warned companies against relying on closed models in a related public post.
- **Why it matters:** Engineering teams that used Claude Code or other external models for internal workflows may need to switch tools or request exemptions while their companies formalize permissible-model policies and data controls.
- **Outlook:** Expect more enterprise bans or restricted-use policies and vendor reviews as firms weigh data exposure and control over model weights.

## UK regulator says it is in an 'arms race' to keep up with AI in financial services and recommends a near-term review

- **What happened:** FCA executive Sheldon Mills said regulators need greater powers to monitor AI use in financial services and recommended the FCA carry out a review within three to six months of AI models used for financial advice and services.
- **Why it matters:** Teams deploying AI for personal finance or automated recommendations should expect regulatory scrutiny, possible new rules, and reviews assessing whether services sit inside or outside FCA jurisdiction.
- **Outlook:** Watch for the FCA-commissioned report publication and any follow-up consultations that could define compliance requirements for model use in finance.
