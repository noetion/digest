Today’s stories focus on tooling and policy that change where and how models run: Google published a native PyTorch TPU backend, DeepSeek and DeepSeek-AI published high-performance inference stacks, the U.S. ordered federal agencies to remove DeepSeek technology, OpenAI acquired Orchestra Bio to extend Rosalind with drug-development agents, and OpenAI published GPT-6 gains on legacy languages like COBOL and Fortran.

## Google open-sources TorchTPU, a native PyTorch backend for TPUs

- **What happened:** Google published TorchTPU on GitHub, a PyTorch backend for TPUs that includes custom ATen kernels, a compilation cache for XLA, distributed training examples, integration with torch.compile(), and a user guide at torchtpu.readthedocs.io; current PyPI release is 0.2.0 and the project requires Python 3.11+.
- **Why it matters:** Engineers can run PyTorch models natively on TPU hardware using the same PyTorch APIs and torch.compile() graph mode, with TPU-optimized kernels and distributed training support, lowering friction for porting PyTorch workloads to Google’s TPUs.
- **Outlook:** Watch for future PyPI releases and updates to the torchtpu.readthedocs.io docs as the project’s compatibility and performance guidance evolves and as PyTorch publishes new version compatibility per the project’s Python Version Support Policy.

## DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, a production-focused inference framework for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash

- **What happened:** DeepSeek released DeepInfer on GitHub, an offline high-performance inference framework that targets DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and supports optimized kernels (FlashMLA, DeepGEMM, DeepEP, TileKernels), attention DP, balanced CP, MoE EP, asynchronous zero-overhead batch scheduling, batch-invariant and deterministic sampling (NVIDIA only), and structured-output constraints.
- **Why it matters:** The repo codifies production techniques for prefill and decode at scale, including decoder SWA bounded replay and confidence-scheduled verification, enabling teams to reproduce DeepSeek’s claimed runtime optimizations and integrate deterministic or batch-invariant inference modes on supported hardware (NVIDIA SM100 and Huawei Ascend 950).
- **Outlook:** Reproducible prefill and decode benchmark runs, measured in the release on 4 SM100 GPUs for prefill and 64 for decode and provided via run_sweep.sh and eval/README.md, are the next public check on the project’s performance claims.

<small>Sources: [github.com](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer)</small>

## U.S. orders federal civilian agencies to stop using and remove DeepSeek technology from government systems

- **What happened:** The Department of Homeland Security directed federal civilian agencies to stop buying DeepSeek products and services and to remove DeepSeek technology, including third-party products built using DeepSeek, citing national security concerns and potential access to sensitive information under Chinese law; agencies may request exceptions to the order.
- **Why it matters:** Federal procurement and existing deployments that use DeepSeek technology or derivatives face an immediate compliance requirement and potential removal, which affects vendors and integrators supplying software that embeds DeepSeek models or runtime components to civilian agencies.
- **Outlook:** Agencies’ exception requests and the resulting approvals or denials will be the near-term public signals showing how broadly the order is applied across existing contracts and third-party products.

## OpenAI acquires Orchestra Bio to add drug-development agents to Rosalind

- **What happened:** OpenAI acquired Orchestra Bio and will fold the startup into Rosalind, OpenAI’s life-sciences platform; Orchestra’s agents track trials, flag timeline impacts (for example a failed production batch), and surface options for next steps to coordinate drug development.
- **Why it matters:** Integrating Orchestra’s agent tooling into Rosalind expands Rosalind’s scope from analysis to operational coordination across trials and downstream decisions, which could automate parts of cross-team and external partner workflows in drug development.
- **Outlook:** Product updates to Rosalind that incorporate Orchestra’s agent capabilities, such as trial-tracking and timeline-flagging agents, are the next concrete milestone to watch for from OpenAI.

## OpenAI reports GPT-6 large-model improvements on legacy languages including COBOL and Fortran

- **What happened:** OpenAI published evaluations showing GPT-6 variants perform much better on low-resource legacy languages: GPT-6 Astra scored 87.4 percent on MultiLangBench across 20 languages (versus about 38 percent for GPT-5.6 Terra) and GPT-6.1 Sol reached 86.6 percent accuracy on a COBOL-to-modern-language migration benchmark at low reasoning effort, improving to 97.9 percent with higher reasoning effort.
- **Why it matters:** Marked accuracy gains on MultiLangBench and the COBOL migration benchmark imply GPT-6 variants can handle codebases and maintenance tasks for legacy infrastructure at higher automated pass rates, which affects modernization, automated translation, and toolchains targeting banking and enterprise systems still running COBOL and Fortran.
- **Outlook:** Further MultiLangBench releases, GitHub updates to the benchmark, and additional published model scores for reasoning-effort variants are the next public checks on these language-specific improvements.
