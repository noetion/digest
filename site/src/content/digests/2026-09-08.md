## GPT-6 Astra completed a full Portal playthrough autonomously in under 24 hours

- **What happened:** A user reported that GPT-6 Astra navigated the entire game Portal and reached the credits without human input after the initial goal, taking about 23 hours and 43 minutes. The run used MCP and a modified SourcePauseTool so the agent receives screenshots, position, and camera angle, chooses inputs during pauses, then resumes the game; code and documentation are on GitHub.
- **Why it matters:** This is a concrete demonstration of a single LLM-driven agent completing a complex, multi-step task end to end, with explicit tooling to pause the environment and feed visual and state context to the model. Engineers building agents or human-in-the-loop pipelines can study the posted code and integration pattern for visual-state sampling and action insertion.
- **Outlook:** Updates or follow-up runs posted to the GitHub repository and additional logs or videos from the same author will be the next public milestones to confirm reproducibility and show engineering refinements.

## Insilico’s AI-designed drug rentosertib shifts six independent aging clocks in a 42-patient trial

- **What happened:** Researchers ran six independent AI aging clocks on blood samples from a 42-patient IPF trial and found treated patients’ protein patterns read as biologically younger than placebo, with strongest effects at three to four years by week 4 and one clock showing up to six years. The drug was developed by AI in about 18 months and is in a Phase III trial for IPF.
- **Why it matters:** Multiple independent AI models agreeing on protein-pattern shifts provides a replicable signal that AI-driven target selection and molecule design produced measurable biomarker changes in this patient set; engineers working on AI-for-drug-discovery should note the end-to-end workflow and the use of external biobank comparisons for benchmarking.
- **Outlook:** The Phase III trial readout for rentosertib is the next concrete milestone for judging clinical and translational impact beyond biomarker shifts.

## Broadcom removed public VDDK download pages, creating migration roadblocks for VMware customers

- **What happened:** Broadcom removed the publicly accessible VMware Virtual Disk Development Kit (VDDK) download pages, returning 404s for VDDK 7, 8, and 9 paths; customers report Broadcom support saying VDDK is no longer available for general use or download. Microsoft updated Azure Migrate guidance to warn access may be restricted and recommend agent-based migration if VDDK is unavailable. Red Hat published a KB noting download errors and said it cannot redistribute the proprietary package while it investigates alternatives.
- **Why it matters:** VDDK is embedded in many agentless migration and migration-tool workflows such as Azure Migrate, Red Hat Migration Toolkit, Nutanix Move, virt-v2v, and nbdkit; removing public access interrupts agentless migration paths and forces reliance on vendor workarounds or agent-based methods, raising migration cost and planning risk for infrastructure teams.
- **Outlook:** A Broadcom public announcement restoring VDDK downloads, an official transition notice, or alternative distribution path from Broadcom are the concrete signals that would resolve the immediate migration barrier; Red Hat’s ongoing investigation into long-term VDDK alternatives is another development to watch.

<small>Sources: [virtualizationhowto.com](https://www.virtualizationhowto.com/2026/09/leaving-vmware-just-got-harder-after-broadcom-pulled-vddk-downloads/)</small>

## Authors report publishers and agents claiming Anthropic settlement payments, triggering disputes

- **What happened:** Authors receiving allocations from Anthropic’s $1.5 billion copyright settlement reported emails indicating publishers or agents made claims on their payments. Under the settlement, authors of nearly 500,000 titles receive $3,000 per pirated work; the payment is split 50-50 with a traditional publisher still in print, or fully to the author if rights reverted before August 10, 2022. Some publishers have told authors these are mistakes and have asked Anthropic to fix allocations.
- **Why it matters:** The dispute process and recordkeeping determine final payment allocation; the reversion cutoff date and publisher involvement are concrete rules that will drive many disputes and corrections, affecting how much individual authors receive and how claims are adjudicated.
- **Outlook:** Corrections from Anthropic and updates to the settlement distribution reflecting disputed claims and publisher fixes are the next concrete milestones for resolving allocation errors.
