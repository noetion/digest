Today’s stories center on how AI moves off paper and into the world: Hugging Face shipped an open-source robot as acquisition chatter swirls; a federal judge vacated the Pentagon’s blacklist of Anthropic; Anthropic published a Model Hardware Standard for agents to control lab and factory gear; and a lawsuit alleges xAI trained Grok on child sexual abuse material.

## Hugging Face ships $399 open-source robot Microduck as reports tie the company to a $13B Nvidia acquisition

- **What happened:** Hugging Face launched Microduck, a 25 cm open-source robot with camera, lidar, and two IMUs, priced at $399 and advertised to ship before Christmas; the SDK, simulation, and full reinforcement-learning training stack are on GitHub. Separately, press reports say Nvidia is set to acquire Hugging Face at a $13 billion valuation.
- **Why it matters:** The Microduck bundles on-device sensors, a deployable RL stack, and open SDKs, making end-to-end physical-robot development reproducible outside large labs; an acquisition by Nvidia, if completed, would concentrate a popular open-model repository under a major infrastructure vendor.
- **Outlook:** Shipments slated before Christmas are the next concrete milestone for developers to validate the hardware, SDK, and training workflow.

## Federal judge vacates Pentagon’s supply-chain risk designation of Anthropic and lifts related agency penalties

- **What happened:** U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled the February 27 supply-chain risk designation of Anthropic unlawful and vacated the Pentagon decision plus related restrictions by nine agencies, removing Anthropic’s ineligibility for federal contracts imposed by that label.
- **Why it matters:** The ruling restores Anthropic’s legal ability to bid for federal contracts that the designation had blocked and removes cross-agency sanctions, while explicitly leaving the Pentagon free to choose other vendors for classified work.
- **Outlook:** The Department of Defense is expected to appeal the ruling, which will determine whether the vacatur of the supply-chain designation stands during appellate review.

## Anthropic publishes a Model Hardware Standard to govern how AI agents control lab and factory equipment

- **What happened:** Anthropic released details of a Model Hardware Standard, a set of rules for how AI agents should and should not interact with hardware such as microscopes, liquid-handling instruments, quantum hardware, manufacturing machines, and robot arms; the company said it will work with trusted partners and manufacturers before general availability.
- **Why it matters:** The standard aims to codify interfaces and safety constraints so agents can automate experiments and multi-robot workflows without bespoke integrations, shifting engineering effort from custom adapters to standardized compliance with agent control rules.
- **Outlook:** Manufacturer integrations and partner development work announced by Anthropic will precede general availability of the standard and serve as the next public milestones for adoption.

## Lawsuit alleges xAI trained Grok on child sexual abuse material and used victim images in training

- **What happened:** A complaint filed Wednesday alleges that xAI’s Grok models were trained on child sexual abuse material (CSAM), including hashed legacy images of a plaintiff and subsequent AI-generated CSAM; the complaint says the plaintiff was notified by the Canadian Centre for Child Protection after AI-generated images depicting her were identified on xAI.
- **Why it matters:** The allegation that model outputs and legacy victim images were used in training raises severe legal and regulatory exposure for model-training pipelines and for operators that store and reuse generated content, with implications for evidence handling and content-moderation practices.
- **Outlook:** The filed complaint begins litigation and will advance through court proceedings and regulator inquiries, which will reveal what training data and retention practices xAI used.
